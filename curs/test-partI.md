**Q11 (4 pts — "Varoufakis would object that…").** A well-known economist writes:

### Section A

**Q1 — (b)** *[L2]*. Surplus is the root; it forces record-keeping (writing) which encodes debt; the storehouse must be guarded and allocated, hardening into the state; ideology arises last to legitimise the distribution. (a), (c), (d) all put a *downstream* thing first — especially (c), which is the debunked barter myth.

**Q2 — (b)** *[L4]*. Yap is the decisive case: the money kept functioning though the physical token was gone and immovable, proving money is the *shared ledger of claims*, not the object. (a) explains why gold is a *good token*, not why money is debt; (c) is false (barter economies are what anthropologists *couldn't* find); (d) is a mechanism, not evidence for the nature of money.

**Q3 — False** *[L1, L5]*. *Why:* markets (marketplaces, one tool among many) are ancient and near-universal; the **market society** (where almost everything, including labour and money, must be bought and sold, and you must sell yourself to live) is recent and was *made* — e.g. via the enclosures. Conflating them is exactly the "human nature" error.

**Q4 — 1:E · 2:X · 3:E · 4:X** *[L3]*. Note the traps: 1&2 are the *same meal* (GDP sees only 2); 3 echoes the water paradox (huge experiential value, ~zero exchange value where abundant).

*Section A scoring: 2 pts each, all-or-nothing except Q4 (½ pt per correct item, rounded).*

### Section B

**Q5** *[L2→L4]*. Look for three genuine links: (1) **surplus** must be tracked among many contributors/claimants → **writing** was invented as accounting (grain receipts). (2) A written record of a *promised future delivery* ("X will deliver 30 measures after harvest") is a **debt** — a claim on the future. (3) When the community outgrows memory, such debts are **tokenised and made transferable**, and a transferable record of debt *is* **money**. *3 pts = all three links; 2 = two; 1 = one.*

**Q6** *[L2+L4]*. Prediction: because money *is* debt and debt carries built-in guilt (*Schuld*), **moral/moralising language will cling to money and finance** — it should feel natural to speak of finance in terms of virtue and sin. Concrete examples (any one): "sound money," "living within our means," "fiscal *responsibility*," a defaulter as a "deadbeat"/"delinquent," "*honest* people repay their debts." *3 pts = clear prediction + valid example; 2 = one without the other fully.*

**Q7** *[L1+L2]*. Both convert a *made, contestable* distribution of the surplus into a *natural, non-negotiable* one so the losers consent. The priest: "the cosmos/gods ordain this tribute." The politician: "no alternative — economic reality ordains this." Same *function* (ideology as naturalisation), different costume (divine will → the will of "the market/reality"). Full credit names the shared naturalising function explicitly. *3/2/1 by how clearly the equivalence-of-function is drawn.*

**Q8** *[L5+L4]*. The two misbehaving fictitious commodities: **labour** → its mispricing yields **mass unemployment**; **money** → its mispricing (of the interest rate/credit) yields **booms, bubbles, and crashes**. The L4 connection: because money is *circulating trust/debt* — a social relation resting on confidence, not a produced good with a stable cost — its "price" swings with sentiment and can be conjured or withdrawn, so it lurches rather than settling to a stable clearing price. *3 pts = both commodities + both diseases + the money-as-trust link; deduct 1 per missing element.*

**Q9** *[L3]*. GDP sums *exchange* values, so it is blind to anything unpriced. (1) **Unpaid human activity**: care work, housework, a grandmother's meal, volunteering — real production and real experiential value, counted as zero. (2) **Nature**: a standing forest, clean air, a stable climate have no price, so GDP records zero for them — and worse, *destroying* them (cutting the forest, selling the timber) *raises* GDP. *3 pts = two clearly distinct blind spots correctly tied to "exchange value only."*

**Q10** *[L2→L5]*. Chain: **surplus** (storable grain) → some people no longer need to farm (fed from the surplus) → division of labour, towns, and eventually owners of the *means of production* (land/workshops) alongside a majority who work → in England the **enclosures** fence off the commons, cutting ordinary people off from the means of their own survival → owning nothing to sell but their labour, they must sell it for wages → **wage labour**. *3 pts = a coherent chain that passes through dispossession/enclosures; 2 = mostly there with a gap; 1 = endpoints only.*

### Section C

**Q11** *[L1,L4,L5]*. Strong rebuttals deploy ≥3:
- **The barter myth**: the "we always truck-and-bartered" premise is empirically false — no barter-first economy has been found; debt/credit came first (L4).
- **Made, not natural**: the market society is recent and *constructed*, not the flowering of eternal human nature (L1, L5).
- **The enclosures**: capitalism didn't "remove artificial barriers" — it *erected* a huge coercive one, dispossessing peasants of the commons by force to manufacture wage labour (L5).
- **Fictitious commodities**: labour/land/money are treated as commodities though never produced as such — the opposite of "natural" (L5).
- **Ideology/self-fulfilling (L1)**: "it's just human nature" is itself the naturalising story — an ideological move that ends argument by declaring the arrangement inevitable.
- **Double movement**: the constant social pushback (unions, welfare) shows the arrangement is contested and requires enforcement, not that it's natural.
*4 pts = ≥3 ideas applied to the quote; 3 = 3 loosely; 2 = 2; 1 = 1.*

**Q12** *[whole of Part I + toolkit]*. This is graded like a mini-capstone. Award up to 10 pts: **2 pts** for correctly reading the speech as ideology (naturalising a political choice as "reality/not politics"); **up to 6 pts** for weaving in ≥5 of the listed tools, each *applied to this speech*; **2 pts** for a coherent, Varoufakis-voiced argument (not a checklist). A model answer would make moves like:
- **Cui prodest?** "Repay our debts" and "no alternative" name winners (creditors, bondholders) and losers (wage-earners, pensioners) while disguising the choice as arithmetic. *(toolkit)*
- **Morality of debt (Schuld):** "honest people repay" weaponises the ancient debt=guilt bundle to shame the debtor nation into accepting punishment — a moral whip, not an economic argument. *(L2/L4)*
- **Ideology:** "this is not politics; it is economic reality" is the priest's move exactly — converting a contestable distribution into cosmic necessity. *(L1/L2)*
- **Money as social relation / self-fulfilling confidence:** "the markets have lost confidence… until confidence returns" invokes a *reflexive* variable; stated by an official it can help *produce* the very market pressure it claims to report — description masquerading as fact. *(L1/L4, previewing L10)*
- **Labour is a fictitious commodity:** cutting wages is *not* like cutting apple prices — it's real human lives, and (preview) my spending is your income, so cutting wages across the board can shrink the economy and worsen the debt-to-income ratio, defeating its own goal. *(L5, previewing austerity in L20)*
- **Experiential value:** the "confidence" arithmetic is blind to the invisible experiential costs — the dignity, health, and futures of pensioners and workers — because it counts only exchange value. *(L3)*
- **Household-vs-nation (preview):** a nation is not a household; "lived beyond its means" smuggles in a false analogy we'll dismantle in Part V.

*Full marks reward integration and specificity, not coverage for its own sake.*
