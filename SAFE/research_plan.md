# Research Plan — EU SAFE Program and Romania

**Date:** 2026-05-22
**Scope:** Objective assessment of the EU's Security Action for Europe (SAFE) instrument with emphasis on costs and benefits for Romania, transparency of contract awards, and the claim that the program disproportionately benefits the German defence-industrial complex (partly as a substitute for an automotive sector under pressure from Chinese competition).

---

## 1. Background — What SAFE Is

Anchor facts to verify and expand during research:

- SAFE = **Security Action for Europe**, an EU defence-financing instrument worth **€150 bn**, adopted by the Council on 27 May 2025, in force from 29 May 2025.
- Mechanism: long-maturity back-to-back loans from the EU to member states, on top of national defence budgets.
- Origin rules: at least **65% of end-product value** must originate in the EU, EEA-EFTA, or Ukraine; contractors/subcontractors must be EU-established with executive management in EU/EEA/Ukraine, not controlled by third countries (with exceptions).
- **Third-country participation:** countries with a Security & Defence Partnership (SDP) with the EU can participate up to 65%. Current SDP holders include Norway, Moldova, South Korea, Japan, Albania, North Macedonia, and the UK.
- Romania's allocation: **€16.68 bn** (second-largest, after Poland's ~€43.7 bn). Domestic legal framework: **Law 4/2026**.
- Romania's split (to verify): ~€9.6 bn military procurement (21 programmes — 10 joint EU, 11 national), ~€4.2 bn strategic highways (A7 Pașcani–Suceava–Siret; A8 Mocta–Iași–Ungheni), ~€2.8 bn Ministry of Internal Affairs / national security bodies.

## 2. Research Questions

### Primary
1. **What does Romania actually get** from SAFE, in money, capability, and industrial offset terms — and at what cost (debt service, opportunity cost, lock-in)?
2. **How are SAFE-funded contracts awarded** in Romania? Where on the spectrum from open tender to single-source negotiation does each major programme sit?
3. **Is the "German industrial bailout" thesis empirically supported?** What share of Romania's SAFE-funded contracts flows to German prime contractors vs. other EU vs. third-country (esp. Korean) suppliers? How does that compare with Poland, Italy, Spain?
4. **What was the Rheinmetall Lynx KF41 IFV decision actually based on**, and why was Hanwha (Redback / K21 / K9 family) excluded from the RFI stage?
5. **What is the Romanian industrial offset?** Local production, joint ventures, tech transfer, jobs — versus pure import.

### Secondary
6. Did the EU's design (65% rule, "design freedom", SDP gating) structurally favour incumbent German/French primes over Korean, Turkish, Israeli, or US suppliers?
7. Is the German automotive-decline → defence-pivot thesis (Rheinmetall, KNDS, Hensoldt absorbing displaced automotive capacity) supported by data on plant conversions, workforce moves, and order books?
8. How transparent is Romania's SAFE governance compared with the EU regulation's transparency requirements and with peers (Poland, Czechia, Baltics)?
9. What is the macro/fiscal impact for Romania — debt-to-GDP trajectory, crowding-out of civilian investment, sovereign rating implications?

## 3. Specific Controversies to Investigate (Named Threads)

Each thread needs primary-source confirmation, a timeline, the named actors, and the strongest counter-argument.

### Thread A — Lynx KF41 IFV award (~€3.4 bn)
- Claim: only Rheinmetall received an RFI; Hanwha and others were structurally excluded.
- Verify: MApN (Ministry of National Defence) procurement notice text, parliamentary defence committee minutes, OUG/HG (emergency ordinance / government decision) authorising the route.
- Compare with: Poland's K2/K9 Hanwha deals (open competition vs. G2G), Australia's Redback selection.

### Thread B — Rheinmetall Skynex SHORAD (7 systems)
- Verify quantities, unit price, lifecycle cost, and whether Israeli (Rafael), Norwegian (Kongsberg NASAMS), or Korean alternatives were evaluated.

### Thread C — PSD "influence peddling" accusations
- Identify the specific PSD statement(s), named officials, the German supplier alleged, and any DNA / parliamentary follow-up.
- Distinguish political accusation from documented finding.

### Thread D — Minister Radu Miruță's "30% price hike at signature" complaint
- Find the original statement (date, venue), which programmes it referenced, and Rheinmetall's response.
- Cross-reference with similar complaints in other SAFE countries.

### Thread E — Transparency carve-outs in Law 4/2026
- Read the law itself. Identify any derogations from the standard public-procurement transparency regime, classification rules, and parliamentary oversight provisions.
- Compare with Romania's pre-SAFE national defence procurement framework.

### Thread F — Romanian industry participation
- Which Romanian state-owned (ROMARM / Romarm subsidiaries — Uzina Mecanică Cugir, Carfil, Tohan, etc.) and private firms have been included as subcontractors / co-producers in SAFE programmes?
- Was any Romanian-led bid rejected? On what grounds?

## 4. Hypotheses to Test (Stated for Falsifiability)

- **H1 (German-benefit):** German prime contractors will capture > 40% of Romania's SAFE-funded contract value by primary award. *Falsified if* the share is below 25%.
- **H2 (Transparency deficit):** A majority of Romania's national-track SAFE contracts (the 11 of 21) are awarded via single-source or restricted procedures rather than open tender. *Falsified if* a majority go to open competition.
- **H3 (Korean exclusion is procedural, not technical):** Where Korean bids were rejected, the documented reasons rely more on procedural / SDP-status / 65%-content arguments than on technical inferiority. *Falsified if* technical evaluation records show genuine capability gaps.
- **H4 (Auto-to-defence pivot):** Major German defence primes have measurably expanded capacity by absorbing automotive supply-chain assets (plants, suppliers, workforce) since 2023. *Falsified if* the expansion is greenfield or unrelated to automotive distress.
- **H5 (Net positive for Romania):** Even accounting for German share, SAFE delivers net positive value to Romania via capability acquisition + infrastructure (A7/A8) + industrial participation, vs. the counterfactual of self-financing the same at market rates. *Falsified if* loan terms + lock-in + foregone competition yield a negative NPV vs. counterfactual.

## 6. Cross-Cutting Lenses

- **Counterfactual:** What would Romania have bought, from whom, at what price, on what terms, *without* SAFE? This is the only honest baseline for "cost-benefit".
- **Capability vs. industrial-policy framing:** The EU regulation explicitly pursues both. Tension between "best kit fastest" and "build the EDTIB" is structural — Romania sits on the receiving end of that trade-off.
- **Geography:** Romania's frontline position (Black Sea, Moldova/Ukraine border) shapes urgency and risk tolerance. A8/A7 motorways are dual-use.
- **Political economy at home:** Coalition arithmetic (PSD–PNL–USR dynamics), CSAT role, presidential vs. governmental authority over defence procurement.

## 11. Notes on Objectivity

- Treat the "German bailout" thesis as a hypothesis to test, not a premise. Some German share is unavoidable given German industrial scale; the question is *how much above what a competitive process would yield*.
- Treat official communiqués (EU, MApN, Rheinmetall) and political accusations (PSD, opposition) with equal skepticism — both have incentives.
- Distinguish three different criticisms that often get conflated: (a) German favouritism, (b) lack of transparency, (c) overpricing. They can be true independently.
- Be explicit about uncertainty. Where the documentary record is thin, say so rather than fill the gap with inference.
