# Research Plan: Economic Effects of the Bolojan Government

**Working title:** *The Bolojan Government and the Romanian Economy: A Data-Driven Assessment*
**Status:** Draft plan — to be refined before drafting the report
**Last updated:** 2026-05-20

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

Romania is in the middle of a heated public debate over the Bolojan government (formed June 2025 under PM Ilie Bolojan, following Nicușor Dan's presidential win). Supporters credit it with stabilising public finances and unlocking EU funds; critics blame it for higher inflation, slower growth, and a squeeze on household incomes via austerity packages ("Pachetul 1" / "Pachetul 2").

This report aims to:

- Separate **measured economic effects** from **political narrative**.
- Cover the period from **government formation (June 2025) to the most recent published data**.
- Compare outcomes against (a) the pre-government baseline, (b) the government's own stated targets, and (c) peer EU economies (notably Poland, Hungary, Bulgaria, Czechia).
- Be transparent about **what is causally attributable** vs. **what reflects broader EU/global trends**.

**Out of scope:** purely political analysis (coalition dynamics, electoral implications, personalities) beyond what is needed to explain policy choices.

---

## 2. Key research questions

1. What were the **stated objectives** of the Bolojan government's economic programme, and how were they justified (deficit pressure, EU procedures, PNRR milestones)?
2. What concrete **fiscal measures** were adopted (VAT, excise duties, dividend tax, public-sector wages, pensions, special pensions, subsidies)?
3. How did **headline macro indicators** evolve: GDP growth, inflation (CPI/HICP), unemployment, real wages, deficit, public debt, current account?
4. What happened to **household purchasing power and poverty/inequality** metrics?
5. How did **financial markets** react: sovereign yields (ROMGB), CDS, EUR/RON, BET index, foreign direct investment flows?
6. What is the status of **Romania's Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP)** and **PNRR (NRRP) disbursements** under this government?
7. How did **business climate** indicators move: industrial production, retail trade, construction, business confidence, bankruptcies/insolvencies?
8. To what extent are observed changes **attributable to government decisions** vs. external factors (ECB policy, EU growth, energy prices, war in Ukraine spillovers)?

---

## 3. Proposed report structure

1. **Executive summary** (one page, data-anchored, no rhetoric)
2. **Context: the inheritance** — fiscal position and macro situation when the government took office (mid-2025)
3. **The policy package** — chronological catalogue of measures with adoption dates and quantified fiscal impact (where official estimates exist)
4. **Macroeconomic outcomes** — GDP, inflation, labour market, external balance
5. **Public finances** — deficit trajectory, debt, financing costs, EDP status
6. **Households** — real wages, pensions, consumption, poverty/AROPE
7. **Business and investment** — industry, services, FDI, insolvencies, PNRR-funded investment
8. **Markets and credibility** — yields, spreads, rating actions, FX
9. **Counterfactual and peer comparison** — what would baseline projections have implied; how does Romania compare to CEE peers over the same window
10. **Attribution discussion** — what can/cannot be credibly pinned on government policy
11. **Conclusions and open questions**
12. **Annex: data sources, methodology, and caveats**

## 4. Public data sources to reference

All sources below are **primary or official**. The report should cite specific tables/series, not just the homepage.

### Romanian official sources

- **Institutul Național de Statistică (INS / INSSE)** — `insse.ro`
  - Quarterly GDP, CPI, labour force survey, industrial production, retail trade, construction, foreign trade, demographics. The **TEMPO-Online** database is the canonical query interface.
- **Banca Națională a României (BNR)** — `bnr.ro`
  - Monetary policy decisions, interest rates, inflation report, balance of payments, external debt, FX reserves, FDI, financial stability report.
- **Ministerul Finanțelor (MF)** — `mfinante.gov.ro`
  - Monthly budget execution, debt bulletin, fiscal strategy, convergence programme submissions.
- **ANAF** — `anaf.ro` — tax collection statistics, VAT gap reporting.
- **Comisia Națională de Strategie și Prognoză (CNSP)** — macro forecasts used in the budget law.
- **Consiliul Fiscal** — independent fiscal council opinions on the budget and rectifications; useful for non-partisan assessment.
- **Monitorul Oficial** — primary source for the actual legal text of each measure (OUG / Lege numbers and dates).
- **Bursa de Valori București (BVB)** — `bvb.ro` — BET index, sovereign bond data.

### European sources

- **Eurostat** — `ec.europa.eu/eurostat` — harmonised HICP, GDP (ESA 2010), government deficit/debt (EDP notification tables), AROPE poverty indicators, labour market.
- **European Commission, DG ECFIN** — Spring/Autumn Economic Forecasts; country reports; EDP recommendations and assessments for Romania.
- **European Commission, RECOVER** — PNRR / NRRP payment requests, milestone tracker, disbursement schedule for Romania.
- **ECB** — Statistical Data Warehouse for cross-country monetary/financial comparisons.
- **ESM / EFSF** publications when referencing EU fiscal architecture (background only).

### International sources

- **IMF** — Article IV consultation reports for Romania, World Economic Outlook database, Fiscal Monitor.
- **World Bank** — Romania Country Economic Memorandum, Doing Business successor indicators, WDI database.
- **OECD** — Economic Surveys (Romania is in accession process), labour and tax statistics.
- **Rating agencies** — Fitch, S&P, Moody's, Scope — Romania sovereign rating actions and accompanying analyst notes (publicly released press releases only).

### Markets / secondary data (for cross-checks, not as primary citations)

- Bloomberg / Refinitiv for ROMGB yields and CDS — when not directly available, BNR and MF bulletins.
- Trading Economics — only as a convenience pointer; always trace back to the primary source.

## 5. Methodology notes

- **Use harmonised series** (Eurostat HICP, ESA 2010 GDP, EDP-basis deficit) for cross-country comparison; use INS/MF national-accounts and cash-basis series for domestic narrative. Note the difference explicitly when both are cited.
- **Time-anchor every claim**: "as of YYYY-MM-DD, series X stood at Y (source, table)."
- **Compare three windows**:
  1. 12 months before government formation
  2. Since government formation (June 2025 onward)
  3. Peer CEE average over the same since-June-2025 window
- **Distinguish announced vs. enacted vs. implemented** measures — many OUG provisions are amended before they bite.
- **Flag revisions**: INS quarterly GDP is routinely revised; use vintages and note the publication date.
- **Avoid spurious precision**: round appropriately; never present forecast figures as outcomes.

---

## 6. Caveats and risks for the analysis

- **Short window**: less than a year of post-formation data limits causal inference; effects of fiscal consolidation typically lag 4–8 quarters.
- **Confounders**: ECB rate path, EU growth, energy prices, regional security situation all move simultaneously.
- **Politicised data interpretation**: both government and opposition will cherry-pick. The report must present full series, not selected months.
- **Definition drift**: "austerity," "real wage," "deficit" are used inconsistently in public debate — define each precisely in the annex.

## 8. Decisions (settled with user, 2026-05-20)

- **Audience**: general Romanian public — educated non-specialists. Avoid unexplained jargon; briefly define EDP, PNRR, ESA-basis vs. cash-basis, HICP vs. CPI, AROPE on first use.
- **Language**: English. Romanian institution names retained in original (with English gloss on first mention), e.g. *Institutul Național de Statistică (National Institute of Statistics, INS)*.
- **Length target**: full report, approximately **30–40 pages** including charts, tables, and annex.
- **Stance**: **strictly descriptive / analytical**. Present data and let the reader judge. No normative claims, no policy verdicts, no "the government should have…" recommendations. Where the data is genuinely ambiguous, say so.
- **Update cadence**: open — to be decided after the first version lands.

### Implications for drafting

- Because the audience is the general public but the language is English, prefer plain prose over equations; use one or two well-chosen charts per section rather than dense tables.
- Because the stance is strictly descriptive, the *Attribution discussion* (§10 of the structure) becomes especially important: it should enumerate competing causal explanations without picking a winner.
- Because the length is substantial, budget roughly: exec summary 1–2 pp, context 3 pp, policy package 4 pp, macro outcomes 5 pp, public finances 4 pp, households 3 pp, business/investment 3 pp, markets 2 pp, counterfactual/peers 3 pp, attribution 2 pp, conclusions 1 p, annex 3–5 pp.
