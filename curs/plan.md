# The Economy Unmasked — A Course

### A guided tour of economics in the spirit of Yannis Varoufakis

> *"I decided to teach you economics precisely because economics is too important to be left to the economists."*
> — the animating idea behind Varoufakis's *Talking to My Daughter About the Economy*

---

## Who is teaching, and how

This course is written in the **voice of a Varoufakis-style teacher** — call him *the Professor*. He is not a neutral textbook. He has opinions, a Greek accent in his prose, a habit of starting with a story before he lets you near a definition, and a deep suspicion of anyone (himself included) who tells you economics is a "science" like physics.

His pedagogy has four commitments, and this whole course obeys them:

1. **Story before jargon.** Every concept arrives dressed as a story, a paradox, or a real historical episode. The technical term is the *last* thing you meet, not the first.
2. **Power is never off the table.** Mainstream courses teach markets as if they were weather — impersonal, natural, apolitical. We will always ask the forbidden question: *who decides, and in whose interest?*
3. **Two questions, always.** For every idea: *(a) How does it work?* and *(b) Who says so, and what would change if we told the story differently?* Economics is partly a self-fulfilling prophecy — the models we believe help make the world they describe.
4. **You argue back.** Varoufakis wrote his introductory book as a dialogue with his teenage daughter, Xenia, who interrupts and objects. You are Xenia. The quizzes are not there to catch you out; they are the interruptions that make the argument stick.

**Language:** all material is in **English**. Ask any time for a Romanian translation (*"tradu în română"*) of a passage, a summary, or a whole lesson, and you'll get it.

## The shape of the course

Seven parts, twenty-four lessons, building from *"what even is an economy?"* up to *"is this still capitalism, and what could replace it?"* Because you already have **some basics** (supply & demand, GDP, inflation in outline), Part I moves briskly and spends its energy re-founding those basics on firmer, more surprising ground rather than introducing them cold.

## The Varoufakis bookshelf (our recurring readings)

We treat these as the course's "set texts." You do **not** need to have read them; each lesson quotes and summarises what we need. But the mapping tells you where to go deeper.

- **T2D** — *Talking to My Daughter About the Economy: A Brief History of Capitalism* (2017). The spine of Parts 0–II and VI. The most accessible; read it first if you read anything.
- **MPE** — *Modern Political Economics: Making Sense of the Post-2008 World* (2011, with Halevi & Theocarakis). The rigorous backbone under Part II.
- **GM** — *The Global Minotaur* (2011). The engine of Part IV.
- **WSWM** — *And the Weak Suffer What They Must? Europe's Crisis and America's Economic Future* (2016). Parts III–IV, the euro.
- **ATR** — *Adults in the Room* (2017). The insider memoir; power and institutions in Part V.
- **GT** — *Game Theory: A Critical Text* (2004, with Hargreaves-Heap). Varoufakis's own discipline; Part V's toolkit.
- **TF** — *Technofeudalism: What Killed Capitalism* (2023). Part VI's provocation.
- **AN** — *Another Now: Dispossessed, Utopia, and What Comes After Capitalism* (2020). The speculative finale.
- **FE** — *Foundations of Economics: A Beginner's Companion* (1998). His critical primer on how mainstream economics reasons — used in Part 0 and as a "method sidebar" throughout.

## The recurring motifs (so you'll recognise them)

Varoufakis returns to a small cast of images. We'll use the same ones, deliberately, so they become mental hooks:

- **The Matrix red pill** — economics as a story that partly *creates* the reality it claims only to describe.
- **Oedipus & the self-fulfilling prophecy** — expectations that make themselves come true (bank runs, booms, busts).
- **Frankenstein & Faust** — technology and debt as powers we create that then escape our control.
- **The POW-camp cigarettes** (R. A. Radford's famous 1945 essay) — how money is *born* out of nothing but trust and convention.
- **Star Trek's replicator** — the post-scarcity thought experiment: if machines made everything for free, what happens to profit, work, and price?
- **The two "misbehaving" commodities — labour and money** — why the things whose prices matter most (wages and interest) are exactly the two the market prices worst.
- **The Global Minotaur** — the mythological beast fed by tribute, used to explain how the US deficit "ate" the world's surpluses after 1971.

## Assessment: how you'll know it's sticking

Three layers, escalating:

1. **Interruption quizzes** (every lesson, ~5 questions). Short, mixed-format — multiple choice, "true/false and *why*", and one open "explain it back to me" prompt. Answers with full explanations are provided in a collapsible section so you can self-mark honestly.
2. **Part tests** (end of each Part, 10–15 questions). These are *synthetic* — they force you to connect the lessons of that Part, not just recall one. Each includes at least one "Varoufakis would object that…" question, where you argue against a mainstream framing.
3. **Capstone challenge.** A single open prompt — *diagnose a real economy (Romania, Greece, or the eurozone) using the whole toolkit* — with a rubric. This is where we can plug into the data and analyses already in this repo (`romania/`, `greece/`, `SAFE/`, `bolojan/`).

Every quiz question is tagged with the concept it tests, so patterns in what you miss point straight back to the lesson to revisit.

## The full lesson map

Each lesson below lists: **the hook** (the story/analogy we open with), **the core ideas**, and **the source**. This is the contract for what each lesson file will contain when we generate it.

### Part 0 — Orientation

**L1. Why economics is too important to leave to economists**
- *Hook:* Why did the British colonise Australia and not the other way around? (Varoufakis's opening gambit — surplus and geography, echoing Diamond's *Guns, Germs and Steel*.) And the Matrix red pill.
- *Core:* What "the economy" is and isn't; the difference between a *society with markets* and a *market society*; why economics is not a value-free science; the self-fulfilling-prophecy problem; how to read an economist critically.
- *Source:* T2D intro; FE.

### Part I — Where economies come from

**L2. Surplus: the seed of everything**
- *Hook:* The first granary. Why agricultural surplus, not human cleverness, gave birth to writing, debt, money, states, armies, priests, and inequality — in that order.
- *Core:* Surplus and its consequences; the co-birth of record-keeping and debt; why hierarchy and ideology emerge to manage and justify surplus.
- *Source:* T2D ch. 1.

**L3. The two values: experiential vs exchange**
- *Hook:* The blood donation that dries up when you pay for it; the friend you'd never pay for helping you move.
- *Core:* Experiential value vs exchange value; why turning everything into a commodity ("marketisation") can *destroy* value; the market's blind spot.
- *Source:* T2D ch. 2.

**L4. Debt came first — and money after**
- *Hook:* There was never a tidy "barter economy" that money replaced (the anthropologists' bombshell). Debt is older than coins.
- *Core:* Debt as the original economic relationship; how money grows out of debt and trust; the moral charge of debt (why we say we "repay" and "owe").
- *Source:* T2D ch. 3; MPE.

**L5. From market*s* to a market *society***
- *Hook:* The enclosures — when land, labour, and even people's time were turned into things to be bought and sold.
- *Core:* The historical rupture that made *everything* for sale; the birth of the two great commodities that misbehave (labour-power and money); why this is the true beginning of "the economy" as we know it.
- *Source:* T2D ch. 3–4; MPE.

*→ **Part I Test** (connect surplus → debt → money → market society)*

### Part II — How capitalism actually works

**L6. Profit and the entrepreneur's gamble**
- *Hook:* The producer who must pay *now* for wages and materials but gets paid *later* by customers who may never come.
- *Core:* Where profit comes from; production as a bet across time; competition as both engine and threat.
- *Source:* T2D ch. 4; MPE.

**L7. Labour: the commodity that isn't**
- *Hook:* You can rent a car and it doesn't care; you rent a person and they do. Why labour won't behave like other goods.
- *Core:* Labour-power vs labour; why wages are not "just another price"; the source of surplus and the tension at the heart of the workplace.
- *Source:* T2D ch. 4; MPE.

**L8. Money as a "misbehaving" commodity**
- *Hook:* The POW-camp cigarettes (Radford, 1945): money invents itself among prisoners, complete with inflation, deflation, and a central bank made of Red Cross parcels.
- *Core:* What money *is* (a social relation, not a thing); the three textbook functions and why they hide the politics; why the "price of money" (interest) is the second commodity that misbehaves.
- *Source:* T2D ch. 4.

**L9. Banks: time machines that borrow from the future**
- *Hook:* The "black magic" — a bank lends money it does not have, and by lending it, creates it.
- *Core:* Credit and endogenous money creation; banks as the mechanism that lets the future finance the present; why this is both capitalism's superpower and its fragility.
- *Source:* T2D ch. 4; MPE.

**L10. Crises: when the prophecy turns on itself**
- *Hook:* Oedipus runs from a prophecy and thereby fulfils it. The bank run that happens *because* everyone fears it will.
- *Core:* Booms and busts as endogenous, not accidental; animal spirits and expectations; why capitalism produces its own crises rather than suffering them from outside.
- *Source:* T2D ch. 4–5; MPE.

*→ **Part II Test** (profit, labour, money, credit, crisis as one system)*

### Part III — Money, banks, and the state

**L11. What actually gives money its value?**
- *Hook:* A €10 note is a worthless piece of cotton — until 300 million people agree it isn't.
- *Core:* Fiat money, trust, and the state; taxes as the thing that anchors a currency's value; the political nature of "sound money."
- *Source:* T2D ch. 4; WSWM.

**L12. Central banks and the invisible hand on the tiller**
- *Hook:* The "independent" central banker who is the most political actor in the country precisely by claiming to be apolitical.
- *Core:* What central banks do (interest rates, lender of last resort, QE); the myth and use of "independence"; who wins and loses from each choice.
- *Source:* WSWM; MPE.

**L13. Inflation, deflation, and the distribution fight underneath**
- *Hook:* Weimar wheelbarrows *and* the quieter cruelty of deflation — two ways the value of money moves, two sets of winners and losers.
- *Core:* What inflation really is; why it's a conflict over shares of the surplus, not just "too much money"; why deflation can be worse; the 2021–23 episode as a case study.
- *Source:* MPE; WSWM.

**L14. A currency without a state: the euro's original sin**
- *Hook:* Europe built a single money but no single treasury — a car with an accelerator and no brakes, shared by nineteen drivers.
- *Core:* Why monetary union without fiscal/political union is unstable; how the euro's design pre-loaded the crisis; the German-surplus/periphery-deficit dynamic.
- *Source:* WSWM.

*→ **Part III Test** (money → central bank → inflation → the euro's design)*

### Part IV — The global machine

**L15. Trade, comparative advantage, and its discontents**
- *Hook:* The Ricardo story everyone is taught (wine and cloth) — and the question the story quietly omits: *comparative advantage for whom, and who gets stuck making the cloth forever?*
- *Core:* Why nations trade; the case for and the critique of free trade; how "comparative advantage" can lock in hierarchy.
- *Source:* FE; MPE.

**L16. Surplus recycling: how a global economy stays upright**
- *Hook:* Every economy runs a surplus or a deficit with the rest; someone must recycle the surpluses, or the whole system seizes. Who plays that role?
- *Core:* Global imbalances; the surplus-recycling mechanism; Bretton Woods (1944) and the dollar's design as the world's plumbing.
- *Source:* GM.

**L17. The Global Minotaur: America as the beast that ate the world's surpluses**
- *Hook:* The Cretan Minotaur was fed foreign tribute to keep the peace. After 1971, Varoufakis argues, the US twin deficits played the same role — absorbing the planet's surpluses and profits.
- *Core:* The post-1971 dollar system; how US deficits stabilised global capitalism; how the beast's "death" in 2008 destabilised it.
- *Source:* GM.

**L18. 2008: the day the machine broke**
- *Hook:* A mortgage in Florida defaults and a bank in Germany collapses — how did a house in one place detonate a system everywhere?
- *Core:* Financialisation, securitisation, and leverage; how the crisis propagated; why it was a crisis *of the system's design*, not a rogue event.
- *Source:* GM; MPE.

*→ **Part IV Test** (trade → imbalances → the Minotaur → 2008)*

### Part V — Instability, crises, and the game of policy

**L19. Game theory: bargaining, threats, and why "rational" isn't enough**
- *Hook:* Two prisoners, two confessions — and why the "rational" move makes them both worse off. Varoufakis's home turf.
- *Core:* Prisoner's dilemma, coordination, credible threats, and the limits of the rational-actor model; why cooperation is fragile and how institutions try to rescue it.
- *Source:* GT.

**L20. Austerity: the doctrine, and the arithmetic that sinks it**
- *Hook:* If a household in trouble tightens its belt, why can't a country? (Because your spending is my income — the fallacy of composition.)
- *Core:* The austerity argument and its rebuttal; multipliers and the paradox of thrift; the moral framing of "living within our means."
- *Source:* WSWM; ATR.

**L21. Inside the room: power, institutions, and the Greek crisis**
- *Hook:* A finance minister sits across from creditors who would rather see his country crushed than admit their programme cannot work. What does this reveal about how decisions *really* get made?
- *Core:* The gap between economic logic and political power; how creditor-debtor relations become domination; a live case study tying together debt, the euro, austerity, and game theory. (Bridges directly to this repo's `greece/` and `SAFE/` analyses.)
- *Source:* ATR.

*→ **Part V Test** (game theory → austerity → the political economy of crisis)*

### Part VI — Machines, the planet, and the future

**L22. The machine question: why bosses fear the robots that enrich them**
- *Hook:* Star Trek's replicator makes everything for free. Paradise — or the end of profit? If robots make everything, who has wages to buy it?
- *Core:* Automation and the paradox of technological unemployment; why productivity gains don't automatically become shared prosperity; Frankenstein and the machine that escapes its maker.
- *Source:* T2D ch. 6.

**L23. Technofeudalism: has capitalism already been replaced?**
- *Hook:* You don't "shop" on Amazon; you enter a private fief and pay rent to its lord. Is profit giving way to *rent*, and markets to *platforms*?
- *Core:* Cloud capital, rentierism, and platform power; the claim that Big Tech has ended capitalism-as-we-knew-it; how to judge the argument critically.
- *Source:* TF.

**L24. The market vs the planet — and *Another Now***
- *Hook:* The market cannot price a stable climate, so it treats it as free — until the bill arrives. Then: a thought experiment about an economy organised differently.
- *Core:* Why markets systematically misprice the environment (externalities, the commons); the limits of market solutions; Varoufakis's speculative alternative (one-share-one-vote firms, markets without capitalism) as a lens on "could it be otherwise?"
- *Source:* T2D ch. 7; AN.

*→ **Part VI Test** (automation → technofeudalism → ecology → alternatives)*

### Capstone

Using the whole toolkit — surplus, debt, money creation, imbalances, the euro's design, austerity, game theory — write a Varoufakis-style diagnosis: *how does it work, who decides, and in whose interest?* We can draw on the live data and charts already in this repository (`romania/`, `greece/`, `SAFE/`, `bolojan/`) to ground the argument in real numbers.
