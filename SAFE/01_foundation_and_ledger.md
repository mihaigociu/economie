## Section A — SAFE Regulation 2025/1106: Key Provisions

### A.1 Identity and timeline

- **Legal act:** Council Regulation (EU) **2025/1106**, "establishing the Security Action for Europe through the Reinforcement of the European Defence Industry Instrument" (SAFE).
- **Legal basis:** Article 122 TFEU (financial assistance in exceptional circumstances) — controversial because it bypasses ordinary co-decision with the European Parliament. The Verfassungsblog commentary frames SAFE as the operational vehicle for "emergency powers move into the realm of security and defence policy."
- **Adopted by Council:** **27 May 2025**.
- **Entry into force:** **29 May 2025** (day after OJ publication).
- **Total envelope:** **€150 billion** in back-to-back loans from the Union to the Member State, drawing on the EU's AAA borrowing capacity.
- **Deadline to request:** Member State expressions of interest were due **30 November 2025** (Romania filed). Single-Member-State procurements eligible for SAFE support must have a signed contract no later than **30 May 2026** (derogation from common-procurement rule; see A.4).
- **Project completion deadline:** Disbursements available through **31 December 2030**; loan tenor 45 years with a 10-year grace period (first repayments ~2035).

### A.2 What SAFE pays for — eligible activities

Two product categories defined by reference to capability priorities the European Council endorsed on 6 March 2025:

- **Category 1** (commodities-style, high-volume): ammunition and missiles; artillery systems including deep-precision-strike; ground combat capabilities (vehicles, soldier equipment, small arms); critical-infrastructure protection; cyber; military mobility (including counter-mobility).
- **Category 2** (capability-system end-items): air- and missile-defence systems; maritime surface and underwater capabilities; drones and counter-drone; strategic enablers (strategic airlift, space assets, AI, electronic warfare).

The Category 2 list maps directly to the heart of Romania's MApN programme set (Lynx, Skynex, Skyranger, IRIS-T SLM, Mistral, NSM, OPVs, H225M).

### A.3 Loan structure (Articles 4–13)

- **Articles 4–8:** financial envelope, request mechanism, loan agreement terms.
- **Articles 9–13:** procedural rules for disbursement — operational arrangements, borrowing/lending, pre-financing, payment and suspension of loans, prudential rules over the loan portfolio.
- **Pre-financing:** up to **15 %** of the loan, available on entry of the loan agreement into force.
- **Allocation cap (Art. 13):** no more than **60 % of the €150 bn envelope** may go to the top three recipient Member States. (The actual top three — Poland €43.73 bn, Romania €16.68 bn, France €16.22 bn — sum to ~€76.6 bn, well under the €90 bn ceiling.)
- **Pricing:** EU credit-quality rates, materially below Romania's sovereign curve. The headline interest-rate differential ("four times lower than current Romanian borrowing" per the PM's office) is a press-quoted comparator, not a contractual term; the actual all-in margin is set per drawdown.

### A.4 Common procurement and the single-MS derogation (Articles 16–17)

- **Default rule (Art. 16(1)):** support is conditional on **common procurement** — at least one SAFE-benefitting Member State **and** at least one other party that is either another Member State, an EEA-EFTA state (Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein), or Ukraine.
- **Derogation (Art. 16(1) second subparagraph):** procurements carried out by a **single Member State** are eligible **provided the procurement contract is signed no later than 30 May 2026**. This is the basis on which a large share of Romania's 21 programmes (the 11 "national" ones, plus de facto the IFV/Lynx, OPVs, Skynex, Skyranger, Piranha V, Iveco trucks) proceed via single-source negotiation with one supplier rather than via genuine multi-country joint tender.
- **Subcontractor definition:** any legal entity that supplies "critical inputs" with unique attributes, is allocated **≥ 15 %** of contract value, and needs access to classified information.

### A.5 Origin and content rules — the "65 % rule"

- **Cost-of-components ceiling:** the cost of components originating outside the EU, EEA-EFTA, and Ukraine **shall not exceed 35 %** of the estimated component cost of the end product. Equivalent to a **≥ 65 % EU/EEA-EFTA/Ukraine value-of-components floor**.
- **Hard-exclusion clause:** "no component shall be sourced from a third country that contravenes the security and defence interests of the Union and its Member States" — the political gating clause used to exclude e.g. Russian/Chinese components categorically.

### A.6 Contractor establishment (Article 16 et seq.)

Contractors and subcontractors must:

1. Be **established** in the Union, in an EEA-EFTA state, or in Ukraine, **with executive management structures** in the same territory.
2. **Not be subject to control** by a third country (or third-country entity) other than EEA-EFTA or Ukraine.
3. Have **infrastructure and resources located** in EU/EEA-EFTA/Ukraine territory (Art. 16(8)).

A third-country-controlled EU subsidiary can in principle qualify only if a Member State provides specific guarantees that conflicts of interest are managed — but a standardised guarantee template is not yet published, creating practical ambiguity (AmCham Romania flagged this gap explicitly).

### A.7 Design freedom (Article 11) — the design-autonomy rule for Category-2 products

For **Category-2 defence products**, contractors must have the **legal authority and technical capacity** to "decide, without restrictions imposed by third countries or third-country entities, on the definition, adaptation and evolution of the design of the defence product procured, including the legal authority to substitute or remove components subject to restrictions."

In plain language: the prime must be able to swap out a US/UK/Korean/Israeli component if a third-country export-licence regime ever blocks it. This is the provision that disadvantages prime contractors whose key subsystems (engines, optics, missiles, datalinks) sit behind a non-EU re-export licence — a structural disadvantage for non-EEA primes (Hanwha, Lockheed Martin, BAE Systems Inc., Elbit Systems Ltd.) seeking to bid as the *prime* on Category-2 SAFE contracts.

### A.8 Third-country / Security and Defence Partnership (SDP) participation

Two regimes:

1. **Full equal participation:** Ukraine and EEA-EFTA states (Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein) — treated as if they were EU Member States.
2. **Participation under SDP terms (up to 65 %):** countries that have a Security and Defence Partnership with the EU and an additional SAFE-specific bilateral agreement. As of mid-2026 the SDP partners eligible to participate include **Albania, Canada, Japan, Moldova, North Macedonia, Norway (also EEA), South Korea, and the United Kingdom**.
3. **Ineligible as primes:** all other third countries (US, Israel, Türkiye, Switzerland-as-non-EEA-for-defence-export, etc.) cannot be the prime on a SAFE-supported contract; their content can fit only inside the 35 % third-country envelope.

The Council concluded a SAFE participation agreement with Canada on 11 Feb 2026 (separately approved in the same Council decision that cleared the first wave).

### A.9 Transparency, governance, reporting (final articles)

- The Commission's services in DG DEFIS act as the lead implementing authority for assessment of National Defence Investment Plans (NDIPs).
- Member States submit an NDIP; the Commission assesses against eligibility, capability, and value-for-money criteria; the Council adopts implementing decisions disbursing the loans on Commission recommendation.
- Reporting on use of funds is annual, integrated with the European Defence Readiness Plan; defective implementation can trigger suspension of disbursements (Art. 12).
- **Transparency caveat:** the Regulation requires reporting **to the Commission and Council**, not to the European Parliament — a recurring criticism, since the EP was excluded from the legislative procedure (Art. 122 TFEU basis).

## Section B — Romania's Law 4/2026 and OUG 62/2025

### B.1 Identity and legislative pathway

- **Government Emergency Ordinance (OUG) 62/2025:** published **20 November 2025**, immediately in force, the executive's instrument to begin SAFE preparations before Parliament had ratified.
- **Law 4/2026:** approves OUG 62/2025 with amendments. Published in **Monitorul Oficial, Part I, no. 10 of 9 January 2026**; effective **12 January 2026**.
- Subsequently amended/supplemented by **OUG 38/2026** (controversially adopted on 8 May 2026 by a caretaker government already dismissed, with a falsified 4 May date and a negative legal opinion — the People's Advocate has filed a Constitutional Court challenge per CriticArad / Defapt).

### B.2 What Law 4/2026 actually does

1. **Designates the institutional architecture for SAFE in Romania:**
   - **Prime Minister's Chancellery (Cancelaria Primului-Ministru):** overall coordination; chairs the inter-ministerial working group that prepares acquisition proposals for CSAT.
   - **Supreme Council of National Defence (CSAT):** approves each acquisition proposal before contract conclusion.
   - **Ministry of National Defence (MApN):** procurement authority for defence programmes.
   - **Ministry of Internal Affairs (MAI):** procurement authority for its tranche (~€2.8 bn).
   - **Ministry of Finance:** manages the loan agreement, drawdowns, debt-service reporting.
   - **Ministry of Economy / Ministry of Investments and European Projects:** industrial-cooperation and localisation policy.

2. **Derogates from Law 98/2016 (public procurement) for SAFE-financed projects:**
   - Adds **Article 4(5)** to OUG 62/2025 (inserted by Law 4/2026): for SAFE-financed or SAFE-financeable **road transport infrastructure projects of national interest**, "the situation of urgency shall be considered demonstrated" — i.e., the urgency justification for accelerated open-tender procedures is *presumed* rather than case-by-case.
   - Specifically modifies the operation of Law 98/2016 **Article 68(1)(a)** (open tendering), **Article 74(3)** (deadline reduction in urgency), and **Article 161(1)** (procedure timelines), shortening minimum tender windows.
   - Exempts SAFE-funded defence procurement from the **offset regulation** (compensatory industrial-cooperation requirements) that otherwise applies to defence acquisitions — a significant departure from prior practice and one of the more material derogations. The CMS Law update is explicit: "exemptions from offset regulations otherwise in place under defence procurement legislation."

3. **Establishes the urgency / classification regime that enables single-source negotiation:** by routing SAFE programmes through the SAFE-specific procurement track, MApN can rely on negotiated procedure without prior publication (the same Article 104 of Law 98/2016 / Article 19 of Law 99/2016 / Article 44 of Directive 2009/81/EC track), which is then formalised by a CSAT decision. The Defence Industry Europe analysis quantifies the result: **9 of the 15 MApN-led SAFE projects sent to Parliament for ratification were awarded via "Negotiated Procedure without Prior Publication"** — the most opaque procedure on the EU spectrum.

4. **Classification:** the law itself does not contain a comprehensive classification scheme for SAFE contract documents, but in conjunction with **Law 182/2002** (protection of classified information) and CSAT decisions, the executive can — and has — classified large portions of contract files. General Dorin Toma (cited in Defence Industry Europe) noted that "contracts appear to have been classified", limiting third-party audit access.

### B.3 Parliamentary oversight — what the law preserved

- Defence acquisitions above a value threshold continue to require a **specific Parliament authorisation** (vote of the joint defence committees of the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate, then plenary). The Defence Ministry on **28 April 2026** lodged a single combined request covering **15 of the 21 MApN programmes worth €8.33 bn**; Parliament approved on **29 April 2026** ahead of the **31 May 2026** SAFE contracting deadline (Reuters via tovima.com, table.media).
- **The 6 remaining MApN programmes** (those below the parliamentary threshold or already covered by prior approvals — Mistral, NSM top-up, the small training/C4ISR/drone items, AHEAD ammunition production via Rheinmetall Victoria SRL) proceed under existing MApN authority + CSAT approval.

## Section C — Romania's National SAFE Plan: Is the Full Text Public?

**Short answer: No — only a partial public release by MApN and Cancelaria, with the full National Defence Investment Plan (NDIP) submitted to the Commission not published in its entirety.**

### What is public

1. **The MApN programme list** (the 21 SAFE_RO_* codes, with most values and broad supplier/country tagging) — published on **mapn.ro/programul_safe/index.php** in January 2026 and reproduced in *cursdeguvernare*, *euronews.ro*, *defenseromania*, and *Defence Industry Europe*.
2. **MApN's English press release "Strategic investments in defence" (no. 150, 10 Sep 2025)** — confirms the €16.68 bn allocation and the broad categories.
3. **The Commission's first-wave press release (IP/26/111, 15 Jan 2026)** — names Romania at €16.68 bn but provides no detail on capability lines.
4. **The Defence Ministry's request to Parliament (28 Apr 2026)** — 15 line items with quantities and values; the document is in the public record of the joint defence committees of the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate.
5. **OUG 62/2025 and Law 4/2026** themselves — fully published in Monitorul Oficial.

### What is *not* public

- **The full NDIP submitted to the Commission** — only summaries.
- **The CSAT decisions** approving individual acquisitions — typically classified at "secret" level; not redacted public versions have been issued.
- **The negotiated contract drafts and the supplier offers** — invoked as classified under Law 182/2002.
- **The Commission's full assessment of Romania's NDIP** — only the IP/26/111 cover communication is public; the technical annex remains internal to DG DEFIS and the Council.

### Identified holders of the full record

The full NDIP and CSAT files have been seen by: Cancelaria Primului-Ministru, MApN strategy/acquisitions directorate, CSAT members (President, PM, Speakers of the two chambers, the heads of the intelligence services, Defence and Foreign Ministers, and the chief of the General Staff), the joint defence committees in restricted session, and DG DEFIS technical reviewers in the Commission. The People's Advocate has been seeking access via the Constitutional Court route in the context of the OUG 38/2026 challenge.

### F.3 What the public record cannot answer at present

1. **CSAT decision rationales** — i.e., *why* Hanwha Redback was excluded at the RFI stage on the Lynx (Thread A of the research plan). The press allegation (Defence Industry Europe; PSD statements) is that the exclusion relied on procedural / origin / SDP-content grounds rather than capability, but the underlying CSAT minute is classified.
2. **The Commission's full assessment of the Romanian NDIP.** Only the cover decision is public.
3. **The actual unit price and lifecycle cost per system** for Skynex (€68M per battery is press-derivable but unit-cost benchmarks vs the Italian deal are not published).
4. **The interest rate Romania actually pays per drawdown** vs. its sovereign curve — i.e., the headline subsidy. Only the broad-brush "4× lower" comparator is in the political record.
5. **The Court of Accounts (Curtea de Conturi) audit status.** No published audit of SAFE-related procurement existed as of mid-May 2026.
6. **Industrial-offset value to Romania.** The "60 % localisation" claim on the Lynx is contested by industry sources at ~35 % (Defence Industry Europe). No methodology has been published by MApN.

### F.4 Cross-cutting caveats on the German-share number

- The 61 % German-prime share **includes** Rheinmetall Italia routings (Skynex, Skyranger, Millennium CIWS). If those were classified by *production country* (Italy) the German share drops by ~€982M to ~51 %.
- The German share **excludes** the German content embedded in non-German primes (Mistral seekers, GM200 radar electronics, the Lynx engine/transmission which are German-supplied to Rheinmetall). If counted at the value-of-content level rather than prime-contract level, the German share is **higher** than the 61 % prime-level number.
- A defensible range for the German economic share of Romania's MApN SAFE pie is therefore **51 % – 70 %**, with **~61 % the most likely central estimate** at prime-contract level.
