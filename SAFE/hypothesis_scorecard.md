# Hypothesis Scorecard — EU SAFE & Romania

**Date:** 2026-05-22
**Inputs:** `01_foundation_and_ledger.md`, `02_ifv_skynex_briefs.md`, `03_political_transparency_briefs.md`, `04_german_pivot.md`, `05_macro_fiscal.md`, `contracts_ledger.csv`.

Falsification thresholds were set in the research plan *before* the data was tallied (see `research_plan.md` §4). Verdicts here are read against those pre-committed thresholds.

## H1 — German-prime concentration

> **Claim:** German prime contractors will capture > 40 % of Romania's SAFE-funded contract value by primary award. *Falsified if* the share is below 25 %.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Verdict** | **Confirmed** |
| **Confidence** | High |
| **Evidence — central estimate** | German primes ≈ **61 %** of the €9.80 bn MApN SAFE ledger (€6.01 bn); Rheinmetall alone ≈ **54 %** (€5.30 bn). |
| **Evidence — range** | 51–70 % depending on how Rheinmetall Italia routings (Skynex, Skyranger 35, Millennium CIWS) are classified. Even on the narrowest German definition (excluding Italia and including only Germany-domiciled primes), the share is well above the 40 % threshold. |
| **Cross-check** | Threads A+B alone (Lynx + Skynex) = ~75 % all-German share of military SAFE. Independently, Hungary ≈ 50%+ to Rheinmetall/KNDS; Poland ≈ 0.4 % (Tusk's figure, verified plausible because Poland has its own PGZ champion). Romania is the *most* concentrated single-prime case in the SAFE pool. |
| **Caveat** | "Prime contractor" is not the same as "value flowing to Germany." Local content offsets (Pirochim JV, Automecanica Mediaș 40 %, UMB Piranha V) reduce the real intra-German economic share by an amount that has not been publicly quantified. |

## H2 — Transparency deficit (procedure mix)

> **Claim:** A majority of Romania's national-track SAFE contracts (the 11 of 21) are awarded via single-source or restricted procedures rather than open tender. *Falsified if* a majority go to open competition.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Verdict** | **Confirmed (strongly)** |
| **Confidence** | Very high |
| **Evidence** | Single-source procedure ≈ **65 % of MApN SAFE value** (10 of 21 programmes). On the 11-programme national track specifically, the single-source share is near-total. The legal hook is **OUG 62/2025 Art. 4(2)** authorising "negotiated procedure without prior publication," subsequently enacted as **Law 4/2026** and extended by **OUG 21/2026** and **OUG 38/2026**. |
| **Cross-check** | Joint-EU track programmes (Mistral G2G, Airbus H225M, NSM Kongsberg, Diehl IRIS-T, WB Group loitering munitions, Thales GM200) follow EDA/multinational procurement and are correspondingly more documented. The opacity is concentrated in the national track. |
| **Caveat** | "Single-source" is not synonymous with "corrupt." For Skynex the catalogue-price benchmark (Italy ≈ €69 m/battery vs Romania ≈ €68 m/battery) suggests sole-source does not always mean overpriced. For Lynx the per-unit price *did* rise +12 % between the 298-vehicle pre-SAFE concept and the 232-vehicle SAFE tranche — a silent price increase masked as a quantity reduction. |

## H3 — Korean exclusion procedural rather than technical

> **Claim:** Where Korean bids were rejected, the documented reasons rely more on procedural / SDP-status / 65 %-content arguments than on technical inferiority. *Falsified if* technical evaluation records show genuine capability gaps.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Verdict** | **Confirmed** |
| **Confidence** | High (within the documentary record available) |
| **Evidence** | (1) No published Romanian technical evaluation matrix comparing Lynx KF41 and Hanwha Redback exists; only Rheinmetall received the RFI. The exclusion was structural — once OUG 62/2025 enabled negotiated procedure without prior publication, no comparative evaluation was triggered. (2) Hanwha's costed counter-pitch (€2.8 bn for 298 units, 80 % localisation, €1.3 bn direct investment, ~9,000 jobs, Petrești facility) was made *outside* a formal RFP, not inside an evaluation Romania conducted and rejected. (3) Australia's LAND 400 Phase 3 (July 2023) selected Redback over Lynx on technical grounds (reliability, tracks, ammunition logistics, blast margin), which removes "Lynx is technically superior" as a defensible Romanian reason. |
| **Caveat** | A defensible non-Korean argument exists on **timeline**: Hanwha's Petrești factory breaks ground in early 2026 with deliveries from 2027, vs. Lynx production lines already running at Unterlüss and via Rheinmetall Automecanica Mediaș. Whether timeline genuinely required excluding Hanwha at the RFI stage, or could have accommodated a parallel RFI, is not documented. |
| **Bottom line** | The exclusion can be defended (timeline, EU/EEA content rules, interoperability with German-equipped neighbours), but it was *not* defended via a published technical comparison. The "procedural rather than technical" framing therefore holds in the documentary record. |

## H4 — German auto-to-defence pivot

> **Claim:** Major German defence primes have measurably expanded capacity by absorbing automotive supply-chain assets (plants, suppliers, workforce) since 2023. *Falsified if* the expansion is greenfield or unrelated to automotive distress.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Verdict** | **Partially confirmed — the pivot is a minority mechanism, not the dominant one** |
| **Confidence** | Medium (well-evidenced; framing nuance matters) |
| **Evidence for** | German auto sector lost ~55,000 jobs in 2024–2025 (trajectory to ~225,000 net job loss vs 2019 by 2035). VW closing ≥3 plants for the first time in 87 years. German defence Big Four (Rheinmetall, Diehl, TKMS, MBDA) added ~16,500 jobs since 2022 (+40 %), planning +12,000 more by end-2026. Documented auto-to-defence cases: **KNDS-Görlitz** (signed, ex-Alstom rail site); **KNDS-Ludwigsfelde** (Mercedes Sprinter plant, pending, ~2,000 workers); **Rheinmetall Berlin/Neuss** internal conversions; **Hensoldt** hired ~200 ex-Continental/Bosch workers; **Renk** recruiting from auto sector. |
| **Evidence against** | The **iconic VW Osnabrück–Rheinmetall conversion deal collapsed in March 2026**. Most German defence expansion is **greenfield** (Unterlüss, Lithuania, Bulgaria, Romania, Ukraine) or brownfield-internal (Hensoldt Ulm, KNDS Munich/Kassel, TKMS Wismar) or M&A (NVL, Loc Performance Michigan, Hagedorn-NC). Workforce absorption ratio caps at ~30 % — defence (~105k jobs) is structurally too small to absorb auto pain (~761k workforce) at scale. **Germany itself did not apply for SAFE**; it funds its own growth via the **Sondervermögen** (€100 bn 2022 + €500 bn 2025). |
| **Reframing** | The "German bailout via SAFE" narrative is **mostly wrong as literally stated** (the auto-pivot mechanism is small; Germany doesn't draw SAFE money) **but partially right in a way that matters** — SAFE is structurally a subsidy to incumbent large EU primes, and those primes are concentrated in Germany (and France). The 65 %-content rule + scale of German primes is the actual economic mechanism. Auto-to-defence is the resonant narrative; prime-concentration is the structural reality. |

## H5 — Net positive for Romania vs. counterfactual

> **Claim:** Even accounting for German share, SAFE delivers net positive value to Romania via capability acquisition + infrastructure (A7/A8) + industrial participation, vs. the counterfactual of self-financing the same at market rates. *Falsified if* loan terms + lock-in + foregone competition yield a negative NPV vs. counterfactual.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Verdict** | **Confirmed — robustly positive across reasonable parameter ranges** |
| **Confidence** | Medium-high (sensitive to SAFE loan-pricing assumptions still being triangulated) |
| **Headline subsidy** | SAFE loan triangulated at ~3 % fixed rate, 45-year maturity, 10-year grace, 15 % pre-financing (~€2.5 bn). Romania's EUR sovereign yield curve sits at **5.5–6.5 %**. Spread therefore = **250–350 bps**. Interest savings during grace ≈ **€420–580 m/year**. Nominal lifetime savings ≈ **€15–21 bn**; PV at 5 % discount ≈ **€5–8 bn**. |
| **Counterfactual** | Without SAFE, Romania would likely have procured **70–80 % of the same capability**, 6–18 months slower, more US/Korea-weighted at the margin, at 5.5–6.5 % financing. Net NPV of SAFE vs. counterfactual: **+€3–6 bn**, dominated by the financing-cost saving, secondarily by capability accelerated. |
| **Costs on the other side** | (1) Debt load: SAFE adds ~4 pp to debt-to-GDP, peaking near IMF's ~70 % projection by 2030; Romania is already under Excessive Deficit Procedure with all three agencies (Fitch / S&P / Moody's) at BBB-/Negative — one notch above junk. (2) Lock-in: Single-source procedures + Cooperation Agreement templates make supplier substitution mid-programme costly. (3) Foregone competition: the silent +12 % unit-price rise on Lynx (and undocumented price deltas on others) is a real cost. (4) Absorption capacity: PNRR (Aug 2026 deadline, €13.6 bn grants), Cohesion (~17 % absorbed of €31 bn), and SAFE compete for the same MoF/MApN administrative throughput. |
| **A7/A8 caveat** | The €4.2 bn highway component is *substantially substitutive*, not additional — A8 and parts of A7 were originally in the **PNRR**, lost PNRR funding in 2025, and have been shifted into SAFE. This raises the SAFE benefit (continuity of strategic infrastructure) but reduces its *additionality*. |
| **Bottom line** | Even with full credit to the German-prime concentration, the silent price hikes, and the absorption risk, SAFE clears the counterfactual bar comfortably. H5 holds. The right question is not "is SAFE net positive for Romania?" but "is Romania extracting the *best* SAFE deal it could have?" — and on that, the answer is "probably not." |
